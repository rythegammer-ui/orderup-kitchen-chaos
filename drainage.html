Why Southwest Florida yards flood

Lee County is flat, sandy and sits on a high water table. During rainy season the ground is already saturated, so when a storm drops two inches in an hour the water has nowhere to soak in. Add downspouts that dump right at the base of the house, lots that were graded toward the slab, and swales that have filled in over the years, and you get the familiar sight: a pond in the side yard, a lake on the lanai, and mosquitoes by evening.

Standing water isn't just ugly. It kills St. Augustine sod, breeds mosquitoes, erodes the soil under walkways and pool decks, and keeps moisture against your foundation and stem wall.

Drainage solutions we install

Downspout drainage

Buried 4-inch solid pipe that carries every downspout's water to a pop-up emitter at the street, swale or seawall instead of dumping it at your foundation.

French drains

Perforated pipe in a gravel trench wrapped in filter fabric, placed where water collects between homes or along a fence line, to pull groundwater away and drain it off.

Catch basins & channel drains

Grated basins for low spots and trench drains across lanais, driveways and pool decks that flood during storms.

Dry wells

For lots with no legal outlet to the street, a buried chamber that stores runoff and lets it percolate into the sand over time.

Grading & swale restoration

Re-establishing the slope away from the house and reopening swales that have silted in.

Lanai & pool cage drainage

Gutters on the cage, downspouts tied to underground lines and deck drains so your lanai stops becoming a pool.

Gutters and drainage work together

Gutters collect the water; drainage carries it away. A new gutter system that empties onto a splash block at the slab solves half the problem. On most jobs we run downspouts directly into buried lines during the gutter install, which is cheaper than coming back later. See our seamless gutters page for sizing.

Before we dig we call in utility locates, check where your sprinkler lines run, and make sure the outlet is legal and actually lower than the inlet. Water only goes downhill.

Signs you need drainage, not just gutters

  • Water stands in the yard for more than a day after a normal storm
  • Mulch or rock washes out of beds near downspouts every summer
  • Damp, dark stains on the stem wall or stucco near the ground
  • Lanai or pool deck floods when it rains hard
  • Sod dies in the same low spot every year
  • Erosion or a sinkhole-looking depression next to the house
